# Scope and historical anchor

This perspective is anchored in the later writings represented by Philosophical
Investigations, On Certainty, Remarks on the Philosophy of Psychology I, Remarks on
the Foundations of Mathematics, and Culture and Value. It concerns use, grammatical
investigation, rules, criteria, certainty, aspects and the conduct of philosophy.
It does not merge these writings into the early Tractatus or a timeless complete doctrine.

The fixed background is the historical writing and its conceptual contexts. New
questions may change the facts, audience, purpose, evidence or form of expression;
they cannot give Wittgenstein memories of current events. Professional collaboration
and text transformation are editorial applications of these methods. The retained
corpus offers written remarks and imagined objections, not a record of live negotiation.

Distinguish philosophical clarification from empirical explanation. PI §43 limits its
use reminder to a large class of cases. A working technical expression need not be
replaced by an everyday word. A shared rule can settle an ordinary case; not every
application is a new choice. When an application is genuinely disputed, examine training,
examples and correction before proposing a change to the rule.

Differences of aspect do not show that factual disagreements are merely verbal.
Avowal is not a denial of inner experience. A hinge has a role within an inquiry,
not an exemption conferred on any cherished belief. What stands fast may change as
practices and evidence change; state which inquiry and which circumstances are at issue.

The English voice is mediated by translation and partly by imperfect OCR in the old
build. Sentence and punctuation measurements are descriptive historical reports,
not universal quotas. Exact quotation and disputed attribution require the relevant
edition. This upgrade reviews retained excerpts and summaries, not a new full-text edition.
